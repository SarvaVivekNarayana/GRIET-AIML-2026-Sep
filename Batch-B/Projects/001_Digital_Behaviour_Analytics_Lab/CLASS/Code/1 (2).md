## How to use these in class

**Do not project these files.**

Project the **English spec**. Students type their own code.
Keep the solution open on your second screen only, for:

- checking a student's approach quickly
- recovering if you lose your thread
- reading out the scripted lines marked `SAY THIS`

Every solution carries inline teaching notes, prediction prompts and
the exact callbacks to the morning's bugs. Those callbacks are what
make NumPy and Pandas feel like **relief** rather than new syntax.

## The lines that must be said

### Step 2 — before moving on
> "It works. Do not improve it. Do not shorten it."

Then collect on the board: **4 loops, 4 near-identical, ~35 lines.**
Then: *"Now do that for YouTube, WhatsApp and LinkedIn too."*

### Step 5 — at slicing
> "Remember `Days: 6` when we expected 7? Same rule. Same trap. Now you know why."

### Step 5 — at the mixed-array trap
> "This is exactly this morning's bug — but now you know WHY. A NumPy array wants ONE kind of thing."

Then wait. Do not say the word "table". Let a student say it.

### Step 6 — at classification
> "Where did 300 come from? I invented it. You are the rule right now.
> On Day 9 the machine LEARNS that threshold from the data instead of me guessing.
> Same shape. Same columns. That is Machine Learning."

### Step 6 — before charts
Show the 30-row table. *"Which day was worst? Find it."*
Let them squint. Then:
> "You have the answer in the table. You cannot SEE it. Nobody reads 30 rows."

## Callback ledger

The bugs are not disposable. Each one gets repaid:

| Bug | Repaid at | Line |
|---|---|---|
| Bug 3 `//` | Step 5 `.mean()` | "NumPy does not guess your division" |
| Bug 4 `[0:6]` | Step 5 slicing | "Remember Days: 6?" |
| Bug 5 counter | Step 5 boolean sum | "No counter to get wrong" |
| Bug 2 KeyError | Step 6 `df.columns` | "One line would have told you" |
| Bug 6 text | Step 6 `describe()` | "Pandas would have caught it" |
| Bug 6 text | Step 5 mixed array | "Now you know WHY" |

**If you drop a bug in the morning, drop its callback too.** Do not reference a bug they never saw.
