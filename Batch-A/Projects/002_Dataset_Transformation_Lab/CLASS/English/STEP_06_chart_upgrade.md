# Step 6 — A Chart Is Not A Message

**Second tool of the day: chart customisation.**

**KEY (from Day 1, reused — not replaced): Picture of the Answer.**

---

## A — Draw the bad version first

Plot all four apps against the day number with no other instructions. Look at it.

Now answer, from the picture alone: which line is Study?

You cannot. Four coloured lines, no names, no title, no units. This is a chart.
It is not a message. Nobody in a review meeting will accept it, and neither
should you.

## B — Four upgrades, one at a time

Add these **one at a time** and re-run after each, so you see what each one buys.

1. **Labels** — give every series a label as you plot it. Loop over your four
   app names, not four variables, and slice a column out of your table each
   time. Four names, one plot instruction.
2. **Legend** — turn the labels into a readable legend. Put it on one row so it
   does not cover your data.
3. **Title and axis labels** — say what the chart is and what the units are.
   "Minutes" and "Day" are not optional.
4. **Annotation** — mark your busiest day with an arrow and a short text label.
   You already computed that day in Step 5. Point the arrow at it.

Add light gridlines last. Markers on the points help when your class is looking
at a projector from the back row.

## C — Second panel: the share chart

Use the percentage table from Step 5 Part D to draw a stacked bar for each day,
one stack segment per app, stacked in order so each bar reaches 100.

Put this panel underneath the first one in the same figure, so the reader sees
raw minutes and proportion of attention in one glance.

Set the y-limit to 0–100 so the eye can compare bars honestly.

**Sanity check:** print the row sums of your share table. Every value must be
100. If a bar is short, your broadcasting direction was wrong — the chart caught
a silent bug that the numbers alone hid.

## D — Save it

Tighten the layout and save the figure as `day02_usage_chart.png` at a readable
resolution.

A chart that lives only on your screen did not happen. The file is the evidence.

---

**Exit condition for this step:** one PNG file exists in your folder, and a
classmate who has never seen your data can state one true fact about your month
from the picture in under ten seconds.
